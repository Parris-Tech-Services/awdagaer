SIGNAL BENEATH

A text-based RPG about family, technology, memory, infrastructure, health, mystery and keeping fragile systems alive

High concept

SIGNAL BENEATH is a large, choice-driven text RPG set in a near-future regional Australian city where ordinary life and an increasingly strange digital world have begun overlapping.

The player is a husband, dad, technician, tinkerer and reluctant investigator trying to keep several systems functioning at once:

- a family
- a home
- a sprawling collection of old computers
- a small technology operation
- increasingly unreliable networks
- unfinished games and software projects
- health and energy
- friendships and work relationships
- forgotten digital history
- a mysterious simulation buried inside local infrastructure

At first, the problems are mundane.

A phone cannot load Google.

Steam flickers on an old laptop.

Windows Update refuses to resume.

An SSD needs installing.

A broken link on a game website leads nowhere.

Someone needs a mosquito repellent.

A certificate file exists but nobody knows the passphrase.

A strange server appears online.

Then patterns begin to emerge.

Devices remember things they should not remember.

Old email accounts contain fragments of conversations nobody recalls sending.

A narrative game called NeathBound begins reflecting events occurring in the real world.

An experimental security simulation named DBO1-SIM appears to know details about local infrastructure.

A collection of ageing DadLAN computers begins receiving messages from a system called THE BENEATH.

And somewhere beneath the city, something is connecting all of it.

The story is not fundamentally about defeating an evil AI.

It is about deciding:

What should be connected?

What should remain private?

What deserves to be preserved?

What should be allowed to disappear?

And:

How do you remain present to the people you love while surrounded by systems endlessly demanding your attention?

1. GENRE

SIGNAL BENEATH combines:

- choice-heavy interactive fiction
- narrative RPG
- mystery
- slice-of-life
- light cyberpunk
- technological archaeology
- family drama
- systems management
- exploration
- psychological mystery
- workplace comedy
- infrastructure simulation
- retro-computing
- soft science fiction

The closest structural influences are:

- Fallen London
- Disco Elysium
- Citizen Sleeper
- Roadwarden
- Suzerain
- A Dark Room
- Choice of Games
- classic browser RPGs
- narrative adventure games with persistent state

But the setting is distinctly Australian and regional rather than metropolitan cyberpunk.

The extraordinary emerges gradually from the ordinary.

---

2. SETTING

Dubbo, 2031

The fictionalised city is known simply as Dubbo.

It is recognisably a regional Australian centre, but the game deliberately exaggerates its hidden technological ecosystem.

Beneath supermarkets, schools, medical practices, council facilities, homes and industrial estates runs a patchwork of fibre, forgotten copper, microwave links, private networks and abandoned infrastructure.

Much of it was never designed to work together.

Now something has made it work together.

The city is divided into several major narrative regions.

---

3. MAJOR REGIONS

HOME

The emotional centre of the game.

Home contains:

- the kitchen
- children's bedrooms
- lounge room
- backyard
- garage/workbench
- DadLAN rack
- router cupboard
- medicine shelf
- family calendar
- piles of cables
- miscellaneous chargers nobody recognises

Many of the game's most consequential choices occur here.

A player can spend an entire evening repairing three machines while technically succeeding at several quests but still leave the household exhausted.

Conversely, the player can choose to abandon a technical problem temporarily and spend time with family.

The game never labels either choice as universally correct.

Instead, consequences accumulate.

---

THE DADLAN

A semi-organised network of old desktops, laptops, switches and improvised infrastructure.

Initially it exists for:

- LAN gaming
- refurbishment
- experiments
- learning
- keeping old computers useful
- local caching
- software testing

Over time, DadLAN becomes one of the primary explorable “dungeons” of the game.

Each computer has:

- hardware
- personality
- quirks
- history
- reliability
- thermal characteristics
- operating system
- storage
- network behaviour
- hidden data

Machines can be physically moved between ports, upgraded, repaired or retired.

Some machines contain fragments of the central mystery.

---

THE NETWORK RACK

Home to several legacy switches.

Notable devices include fictionalised equivalents of:

- a quiet 100 Mbps unmanaged switch
- a D-Link smart Gigabit switch
- a Cisco Catalyst-era switch
- an HP ProCurve-class enterprise switch

The player initially learns a simple lesson:

Fast switches tend to have fans.

Later this becomes symbolic.

High-performance systems create noise.

Quiet systems create limits.

The player repeatedly encounters versions of this trade-off across technology, work and personal life.

---

THE DATA CENTRE

A highly secured regional facility operated by Leading Edge Infrastructure.

Inside it exists a training simulation:

DBO1-SIM

Employees use the simulation to understand how small security failures can combine into larger incidents.

Within the fictional game, DBO1-SIM contains abstracted mechanics involving:

- trust
- access
- physical security
- identity
- social engineering
- network segmentation
- privileges
- detection
- incident response

The gameplay deliberately avoids reproducing real operational intrusion procedures.

Instead, security actions work like RPG abilities.

Example:

«OBSERVE ACCESS ROUTINE
Difficulty: Watchful 32
Success: Gain “Pattern of Trust”
Failure: Suspicion +1»

Later, DBO1-SIM begins generating scenarios nobody programmed.

---

DUBBO HEALTH

A large hospital precinct containing multiple departments, corridors and confusing building names.

Finding something as mundane as Staff Health becomes an early navigation quest.

The hospital later becomes important because medical devices begin producing unusual network signatures.

Health-related quests remain grounded rather than magical.

The player's body exists as a real system.

Sleep matters.

Illness matters.

Medication matters.

Stress matters.

Breathing matters.

But the game deliberately avoids turning health into simplistic numerical optimisation.

---

THE CHEMIST

A surprisingly important location.

Here the player deals with:

- medicines
- cough treatment
- skincare
- mosquito protection
- children's products
- ingredient labels
- competing product claims

The Chemist introduces the game's Evidence vs Marketing mechanic.

Products may advertise themselves as:

NATURAL

CLINICAL

DERMATOLOGICALLY TESTED

EXTRA STRENGTH

FAMILY SAFE

None of these words automatically determine whether something is appropriate.

Players inspect actual ingredients and context.

This teaches a larger narrative principle:

Labels are not evidence.

---

THE MEDICAL PRACTICE

A community medical practice running increasingly fragile administrative systems.

One quest involves a digital certificate file whose identity information is known but whose accompanying credentials and configuration have become unclear.

The storyline explores:

- institutional knowledge
- documentation
- certificates
- identity
- responsibility
- “the person who used to know how this worked”
- systems that function until the one employee who understands them leaves

No actual credential recovery techniques are given.

The problem is represented as a documentation and governance puzzle.

---

THE WORKSHOP

A technology repair and support environment.

Players handle jobs such as:

- DNS failures
- Wi-Fi issues
- Windows Update
- hardware upgrades
- old PCs
- software configuration
- user accounts
- certificates
- network troubleshooting

Some jobs can be solved quickly.

Others reveal deeper systemic issues.

The most important skill is often not technical expertise.

It is correctly determining:

What problem are we actually solving?

---

THE ARCHIVE

The Archive is a surreal representation of the player's digital history.

It contains:

- Gmail
- Outlook
- old university accounts
- abandoned addresses
- Google Drive
- old documents
- forgotten folders
- ancient messages
- recovered files
- dead podcast links
- photos
- game projects
- fragments of conversations

Rooms are organised chronologically.

Descending deeper takes the player further into the past.

A mysterious folder labelled:

2004

exists near the lowest accessible level.

---

NEATHBOUND

A fictional browser RPG being developed by the player.

Initially it is simply another unfinished project.

It contains:

- storylets
- locations
- qualities
- challenge rolls
- menace systems
- choices
- consequences

Eventually the player discovers that events in NeathBound sometimes change before they are coded.

A character may mention a broken laptop.

A location may suddenly resemble a hospital corridor visited earlier that day.

A storylet may contain a phrase from a private email.

At first this could be coincidence.

Eventually it cannot.

4. PLAYER CHARACTER

The player character can be renamed, but the default role is:

THE KEEPER

A person who keeps things going.

Computers.

Networks.

Family routines.

Old devices.

Projects.

Memories.

Relationships.

Systems.

The title begins as affectionate.

Later it becomes a warning.

Because keeping everything alive indefinitely is impossible.

5. CORE ATTRIBUTES

The game uses six primary attributes.

WATCHFUL

Observation, technical diagnosis and pattern recognition.

Used for:

- troubleshooting
- reading logs
- noticing inconsistencies
- analysing systems
- investigating mysteries

---

PRACTICAL

Ability to physically fix, configure and implement.

Used for:

- SSD installs
- network changes
- repairs
- configuration
- infrastructure

---

PERSUASIVE

Communication and relationships.

Used for:

- helping users
- family conversations
- negotiating access
- explaining technical issues
- asking difficult questions

---

STEADY

Emotional regulation and ability to remain present under pressure.

Used during:

- conflict
- illness
- parenting
- outages
- high-stakes troubleshooting

Steady is not “suppress emotion”.

It represents the ability to remain connected to what is actually happening.

---

CURIOUS

Exploration, research and experimentation.

High Curiosity unlocks strange pathways.

It also creates risk.

A player with extreme Curiosity may continually begin new projects while leaving existing responsibilities unfinished.

---

DISCERNMENT

The ability to determine what matters.

This is intentionally the hardest attribute to increase.

Discernment affects choices like:

«Continue debugging?»

«Go to bed?»

«Repair another laptop?»

«Read to your child?»

«Search for one more clue?»

«Accept that some information cannot be recovered?»

---

6. SECONDARY METERS

ENERGY

Affected by sleep, illness, workload and stress.

Low Energy changes dialogue and challenge difficulty.

---

ATTENTION

A finite daily resource.

Technical rabbit holes consume it rapidly.

---

FAMILY CONNECTION

Represents accumulated relational presence.

It cannot be farmed mechanically.

Repeated shallow interactions provide diminishing returns.

---

SYSTEM STABILITY

Measures the overall health of your technical environment.

High stability reduces emergency events.

---

TECH DEBT

Bad fixes create Tech Debt.

Example:

You can repair DNS quickly by hard-coding settings.

The immediate problem disappears.

Three chapters later another device fails because the workaround became permanent.

---

TRUST

Tracked separately with:

- family
- colleagues
- clients
- institutions
- AI systems

---

NOISE

One of the game's central hidden variables.

Noise includes:

- alerts
- unfinished tasks
- notifications
- physical fan noise
- emotional tension
- cognitive clutter
- unreliable systems

Some upgrades increase performance while increasing Noise.

MORNING

Review:

- household status
- sleep
- weather
- messages
- work tickets
- device alerts

Choose priorities.

---

DAY

Workplace and city quests.

Examples:

- diagnose a machine
- visit the hospital
- research equipment
- solve network faults
- follow an archive clue
- investigate DBO1-SIM

---

EVENING

Home decisions.

Possible actions:

- family time
- DadLAN repairs
- gaming
- project development
- research
- rest
- exercise
- household jobs

---

NIGHT

Night actions are unusually powerful.

You can:

«KEEP WORKING»

9. HARDWARE PROGRESSION

Machines can receive:

- SSDs
- RAM
- operating system upgrades
- driver repairs
- network improvements
- cooling work
- software optimisation

The player learns to allocate scarce upgrades intelligently.

Installing the best SSD in the weakest machine may be less useful than upgrading a system close to becoming viable.

Hardware choices therefore resemble RPG equipment allocation.

10. NETWORK GAMEPLAY

Networking is represented visually through text diagrams.

Example:

INTERNET
│
ROUTER
│
DADLAN CORE
├── LAPTOP 03
├── LAPTOP 06
├── CACHE NODE
└── UNKNOWN DEVICE

DNS becomes one of the game's recurring motifs.

DNS determines where names lead.

Later this becomes philosophically significant.

If a system controls how names resolve, it can change where reality appears to be located.

---

11. THE LANCACHE INCIDENT

One major early quest involves building a local content cache for games.

Objective:

Reduce repeated downloads across DadLAN.

The player changes network-wide DNS settings too early.

Suddenly:

- Android phones lose internet access
- Chrome fails
- Google refuses to load
- laptops behave differently

The player must diagnose the failure.

The permanent lesson becomes a gameplay rule:

SAFE DEPLOYMENT

Before changing infrastructure globally:

1. verify the new service
2. test ordinary DNS forwarding
3. test one client
4. test a mobile device
5. confirm rollback
6. deploy gradually

Completing this quest unlocks:

CHANGE CONTROL

which reduces future network-wide disasters.

---

12. THE GOOGLE THAT DISAPPEARED

A recurring mystery quest.

Chrome appears broken.

But Chrome is not the actual problem.

The player must distinguish:

APPLICATION

from

NETWORK

from

DNS

from

VPN

from

ROUTER

The lesson:

The visible failure is not necessarily the originating failure.

This principle later becomes essential to the central mystery.

---

13. THE QUIET SWITCH

The player compares several network switches.

The quiet old switch provides only 100 Mbps.

The louder enterprise equipment offers far greater throughput.

This becomes a repeating metaphor.

Several later choices resemble it:

«Faster job / more stress»

«Bigger network / more maintenance»

«More projects / less presence»

«More performance / more heat»

There is no permanent “correct” answer.

---

14. WINDOWS AS A DUNGEON

Windows troubleshooting becomes an almost literal dungeon.

Players encounter layers:

SETTINGS

POLICY

REGISTRY

SERVICE

UPDATE CACHE

DRIVER

NETWORK STACK

A button labelled RESUME UPDATES may do nothing because a deeper policy says:

NO AUTO UPDATE.

The game teaches the player not to trust interface-level appearances.

---

15. THE FLICKERING STEAM QUEST

On Laptop 06, Steam becomes visually unstable.

The player finds a workaround.

The important follow-up quest is not:

“Can you make it work?”

It is:

“Can you make the fix persist?”

This introduces:

PERSISTENCE

Temporary fixes do not count as completed quests unless explicitly accepted as temporary.

---

16. THE PASSWORD-RESET USB

The player needs a legitimate recovery environment for an old computer.

A download link returns:

404.

This begins a recurring theme:

The internet forgets.

Old documentation disappears.

Downloads vanish.

Links rot.

Files survive without context.

This eventually connects to the Archive storyline.

---

17. THE ARCHIVE SYSTEM

The player can perform increasingly deep searches across their digital history.

Search modes include:

KNOWN NAME

DATE RANGE

EXACT PHRASE

ACCOUNT

FILE TYPE

RELATIONSHIP

SOURCE

The Archive contains both valuable history and false leads.

Some addresses look familiar but belong to someone else.

The player can mark evidence:

CONFIRMED

PROBABLE

UNVERIFIED

FALSE ATTRIBUTION

This classification mechanic becomes crucial later.

---

18. MEMORY AS A GAME SYSTEM

The game strongly distinguishes:

REMEMBERING

from

VERIFYING

Characters may say:

«“We talked about this before.”»

The player's journal may remember fragments.

But the player cannot automatically claim certainty.

This leads to a mechanic called:

EVIDENCE OF MEMORY

Possible response choices include:

«I remember generally.»

«I found the original record.»

«I couldn't locate it.»

«I may be mixing two conversations together.»

Honest uncertainty usually strengthens long-term Trust.

Pretended certainty may solve a dialogue challenge immediately but creates future consequences.

---

19. THE LOST CHAT

A conversation called:

UNIQUE LOCATION ARTWORK DONE

refuses to open.

The interface displays:

COULD NOT LOAD THIS CONVERSATION.

The player must reconstruct the project's state from:

- commits
- screenshots
- surviving assets
- deployed builds
- other chats
- repository history

This becomes a miniature version of the central plot.

How much of a thing survives when its original narrative disappears?

---

20. NEATHBOUND

NeathBound is the player's main fictional game project.

It contains:

- atmospheric locations
- storylets
- qualities
- challenges
- rewards
- menace systems
- progression

Initially it has several bugs.

Examples:

A choice displays:

NO CHALLENGE ROLL

but secretly performs one.

A failed check gives the success reward.

Two systems modify different versions of DREAD.

A polished UI hides shallow progression.

Fixing NeathBound teaches the player about:

CONSISTENCY

The story the interface tells must match what the underlying system actually does.

Later the real world begins violating the same principle.

21. THE MISSING ART

Several beautifully illustrated locations vanish after a deployment.

The website still runs.

Technically the build passes.

But the experience is worse.

This introduces a key design philosophy:

Passing tests is not identical to working correctly.

The player gains:

VISUAL VERIFICATION

and later:

PLAYER EXPERIENCE TESTING.
